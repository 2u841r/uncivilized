Over 5 million Algerians were killed by French colonialism. This is what the Algerian struggle for liberation can teach us about Palestine, and how colonial violence is used to overwhelm, deter, and completely paralyze demands for freedom. Algeria was a busy piece of metropolitan France transplanted across the Mediterranean. France colonized Algeria in 1830. It wasn't just a colony: French Algeria legally became part of France. Think the US and Hawaii. And like all cases of colonialism, there was an ideology and narrative justifying it. By law, Algerian natives were pushed off their land to make way for these European settlers. Sound familiar? These settlers were called pieds noirs, or black feet, and by 1962 there were almost 1 million of them. In the 132 years of French colonization, generation after generation was born there. They developed their own identity, culture, and peoplehood as French Algerians, distinct from France but also from the native Algerians. Algeria was the only home they knew. And in this reality there was a fundamental inequality between the settlers and the Algerians, and France used cruel and horrific violence to rule Algeria, control its people, and maintain this regime. While there was resistance to French colonialism from the onset, a major turning point happened in 1945. Thousands of Algerians took to the streets to demand independence. Police fired at them, which in turn led to attacks on settlers, killing 102. The French authorities and pied noir militias responded by killing 45,000 Algerians in the span of 2 weeks. Because how dare the inferior barbarian native demand freedom from us enlightened Europeans? Violence was used as retribution, revenge, and repression to remind Algerians who was in charge. Needless to say, Israel is doing the same in Gaza. The scale of violence we are seeing and the mass killing of Palestinians is this very colonial logic, because if it was really about the hostages, the deal was on the table early on to release them in exchange for a ceasefire. Thousands of lives would have been saved, but Israel rejected this. Why? Israel is more interested in reestablishing deterrence, which in other words means a scale of calamity and violence that will prevent and paralyze any type of resistance to its colonial rule in the future. And under this pretext it is further colonizing Palestine. So what ended up happening in Algeria? Like in South Africa, Vietnam, and Ireland, Algerians determined that France was never going to kindly give them their independence. They had to take it. In 1954 they launched their struggle for liberation, which included armed resistance. And as expected, France unleashed unspeakable repression and violence, that included mass torture of hundreds of thousands of people, often through rape. "It was agreed that we would stop that strike at any price, you understand, at any price." That price included degrading forms of torture practiced by General Massu's parachute division as they swept the streets of Algiers' ancient Muslim quarter, the Casbah, and the killing of 1.5 million Algerians in 8 years. A combination of sustained guerrilla warfare, internal French divisions with the fall of the Fourth Republic, and the internationalization of the conflict by Algerians to gain global support intensified the pressure on France. Following a negotiated ceasefire and a referendum for self-determination, Algeria gained its independence in 1962. And after that, 800,000 settlers left for France and the settler colonial rule ended. French violence failed to deter, overwhelm, or paralyze the Algerian appetite for liberation. This is the lesson to be learned: like France, Israel will also fail. Its mass atrocities will never suppress the Palestinian appetite for liberation and self-determination, no matter how cruel it is. It can't reestablish deterrence. And during these dark and bleak times, it is important to look to history for perspective and guidance that can shine light on a brighter future.
