AI went from making weird and funny pictures to, what feels like overnight, taking over my feed. "AI influencers are coming fast." "There's a driverless damn car. Nobody in the car, with a billboard that says humans are so 2023." "OMG people, the world is ending. Are you seeing this? This is actually so exciting." "Every day AI can do more and it's raising more concern." It promises open knowledge and boundless creativity. But whose knowledge, and at what cost? "Honestly, it's like modern-day slavery." "In many industries, the dirty work is done by people from the global south." "Burning enough methane to power a small city with no permits and no pollution controls. The race for AI dominance is a race to the bottom for safety." "All while the companies and people accelerating this work line their pockets and become more and more out of touch and untouchable. Surveillance capitalism, the AI warlord. It's oligarchy." "Capitalism is dead. Welcome to technofeudalism." There are a lot of terms and frameworks to make sense of all this, but the framing of one journalist in particular is what will guide us to understand what's happening in the world of AI today: "An enormous consolidation of economic and political power in the hands of these companies. These companies actually need to be thought of as new forms of empire." ## The Resource Grab Empires need resources to sustain themselves and grow bigger. Silicon Valley has always tried to make themselves appear magical by hiding the physical supply chain, and while AI feels suspended in servers and clouds, it's actually very resource hungry. First, there's the amount of land they take up. "When people talk about the cloud, this is kind of where we are now." "This is what they're talking about. It's not in the sky. It's right here. When you ask ChatGPT a question, the answer is being processed and sent back to you in a place like this. It's called a data center. Everything we do online goes through them, so they're not new. This one is from 2014. But running AI requires a lot of computing power." And so the AI boom has triggered a race to build way more data centers, faster, and much bigger: "$15 billion." "$16 billion." "$20 billion." And then there's the energy needed. "The turbines were capable of generating about 420 megawatts of electricity, enough to power around 300,000 homes." With these machines running 24/7, they're prone to overheating, so these data centers need to be cooled as well. "For liquid cooling, the water must be clean, which means using mostly drinking water." "This is our cold water pressure in the kitchen." It's overwhelming because you really feel like you are up against this huge wall that you can't penetrate. These data centers disrupt local power grids and put incredible strain on peripheral areas. Not to mention the amount of light, noise, and air pollution that comes from one, building these mega campuses, and two, running them. Elon Musk installs quick and dirty turbines to power xAI's Memphis data centers. "They don't have a problem with selling our land, selling our, selling our lives." "Let's stop now." And then there's the minerals and mining and manufacturing to make all the equipment inside these buildings: the thousands of GPUs, whose models are being upgraded and accelerated every 2 years. There's another kind of resource too, one that's not tangible, but definitely mined and exploited. In the past, empires expanded by seizing land. Today's empires expand by seizing data. "It's always the data. It's the crack cocaine of Silicon Valley." "Every organization wants to implement AI, but oftentimes the biggest bottleneck in their way is being able to create really high quality data and data sets to power that AI." These AI models are trained on the work of artists and writers, and then on our information too: every click, every image, every voice, turned into digital territory, and all of that is scraped from the internet without our permission or compensation. This has often been called data colonialism. All that data needs to be compiled and refined by people first before being fed to these machines. Those people have often been in the global south, like in Venezuela and Kenya: well educated but unemployed. "It's not because you realize Kenya is a third world country. You say this job I would normally pay $30 in US, but because you are Kenya, $2 is enough for you. That idea has to end." Another American AI training company facing criticism in Kenya is Scale AI. Sometimes the company just didn't pay them. "When it gets to the day before payday, they close the account and say that you violated a policy." This guy's name is Alexander Wang. He's the CEO of Scale AI. ## Monopoly and the Race to AGI Just like empires, these companies are gobbling up resources, selling it back to those pillaged and robbed on the margins, and turning it into wealth for themselves, all the while selling the idea that we need to build and accelerate towards bigger and better AI. And ultimately all empires have this narrative: that there are evil empires and there are good empires, and the reason why they, the good empire, have to be an empire in the first place and engage in all this resource and power consolidation is because they must be strong to beat back that evil empire. The goal is to be on top. That means constant expansion and competition. Just like empires of the past were competing against one another, so are these tech giants. There was this aggressive race of: we, the French Empire, are better than the British Empire. We, the British Empire, are better than the Dutch Empire. And we need to continue to relentlessly race and be number one because we're the ones that have the right civilizing mission to bring modernity and progress to all of humanity. And where their missions might have once been about scientific exploration and transparency, they are now about profit, which has resulted in a landscape where they are just scaling at all costs. And they're all trying to be the first to build AGI, artificial general intelligence, which is AI that can understand and learn anything that a human can. "Two different factions called the boomers and the doomers. Boomers believe that AGI is going to bring utopia. Doomers believe AGI is going to kill all of humanity. I consider them two sides of the same coin, and that ultimately they believe fervently in the idea that it is possible to recreate human intelligence. We don't actually have any scientific evidence that that is possible. It is a belief, and they themselves talk about it as a belief. And if you believe that this will lead to civilizational transformation, that is why they conclude they need to be the one to carefully control the development." This is being called the AI race for a reason. Here's Sam Altman, now CEO of OpenAI, introducing his mentor, Peter Thiel, founder of Palantir: "If you're starting a company, if you're the founder, entrepreneur starting a company, you always want to aim for monopoly, and you want to always avoid competition, and so hence, competition is for losers." This is the ideology of empire. Eliminate competition, consolidate control, and write the rules for everyone else. First comes monopolizing the production of knowledge around AI. "So most of the AI research is actually filtered through the lens of what is good for these companies versus not, because they have a monopoly on the top AI talent in the world." Then comes monopolizing the truth. Because if AI is the aggregate of all human intelligence, then deciding what information is shared is a lot of power. This is also a standard empire move: control the archive, control the narrative. And they're monopolizing our attention. "We used to colonize land. That was the thing you could expand into, and that's where money was to be made. We colonized the entire earth. There was no other place for the businesses in capitalism to expand into. And then they realized human attention. They are now trying to colonize every minute of your life. Every single free moment you have is a moment you could be looking at your phone, and they could be gathering information to target ads at you." Do you know what Netflix's CEO said the app's biggest competitor is? Sleep. Tech oligarchs see our collective attention as their manifest destiny, and I'm making that comparison very deliberately. Culture now is just what's next on your phone, and that's AI. Culture is AI now. And all this with no transparency on what is fed into these AI models. That's why the Chinese AI company DeepSeek was such a threat. "Last week, Chinese company DeepSeek shocked the world when they walked right over OpenAI's moat by releasing the open-source R1 model, and it went viral to become the number one app in America." Not only was it better than ChatGPT, but it only cost around $6 million to develop, about 10% of what OpenAI spent on GPT-4, and it used fewer and less advanced GPUs. And it's open source, meaning unlike Silicon Valley companies, its code is freely available and its development is transparent. "This is a big problem for big tech, because in order for the grift to keep on grifting, artificial intelligence needs to be hard and take thousands of GPUs to pull off. And that's terrifying if you're a tech investor pouring billions of dollars into AI. Because if the best state-of-the-art model can operate on relatively cheap consumer hardware, and it's free and open source, and developed in China, how are we going to make any money?" This of course was not good for the empires of Silicon Valley. So now the AI race has become an AI war, complete with the typical empire fear-mongering talking points. ## The AI War "In the tech industry, we often talk about missions. They're often frivolous. This mission, on the other hand, really matters. The AI war will define the future of our world and has the potential to shift the balance of diplomatic power." If what is good for these companies is profits and the bottom line, then it is no surprise that we're now seeing collaborations with one of the most insatiable and ever-expanding industries on Earth. "Well, the tech squad is reporting for duty. Executives from OpenAI, Palantir, and Meta are on a mission, swapping their swanky offices for military bases with a goal of bringing some much-needed tech upgrades to the army." The use of AI in warfare isn't hypothetical or science fiction. It's already live. Israel is increasingly using AI in its genocide of Palestinians, like the two systems revealed in this investigation, called Project Lavender and Where's Daddy. The first uses AI to create a kill list marking tens of thousands for assassination. The only human intervention is to confirm that the target is a man. The second is an AI system that tracks these men and signals when they've entered their homes, because, quote, "It's much easier to bomb a family's home. The system is built to look for them in these situations." Israel does this with the support of Amazon's cloud service and AI tools from Google and Microsoft. For them, it's a laboratory. Of course, they want to work with us. Just as past empires used colonies as laboratories, so too does the AI empire today. "How about you, Sean? Those alien war crimes are powered by Azure." "You have blood on your hands. All of Microsoft has blood on its head." "Google, you can't hide. You are funding genocide. I'm a Google Cloud software engineer and I refuse to build technology that powers genocide, crime, or surveillance." These companies are acting across the world, playing a role in global politics, in genocide, and they're racing to AGI, developing a tool that might pose an existential risk to humanity, with no transparency and a priority of power and profit over our collective well-being. "Because they don't really care how much cost it takes on the current planet, because they're done with it. They're just trying to, while this earth still exists, pump as much of the resources as possible." "Exactly." ## Regulation and Resistance So who do they answer to? Who is holding them to account? Big tech often frames regulation as a threat to innovation. "The ones that are massively regulated are not happening. The ones that are unregulated are happening." But behind the scenes, these companies are helping shape the laws that are meant to restrain them: "We believe that excessive regulation of the AI sector could kill a transformative industry just as it's taking off." "As Musk helps dismantle the very federal agency that's supposed to protect us against pollution like this, there is no way to miss how this is a coordinated strategy." "It's about unleashing energy dominance, making America the AI capital of the world, and the EPA can't be restrictive of that. We can't be suffocating it." "We could never win an election on getting certain things because we were in such a small minority. But maybe you could actually unilaterally change the world without having to constantly convince people and beg people and plead with people who are never going to agree with you, through technological means. And this is where I think technology is this incredible alternative to politics." We are rapidly recreating a time where there is a small group of people that get to make all of the decisions and they act completely in self-interest and everyone else kind of lives with the ramifications of their whims. ## Another Path AI doesn't have to go down a path of doom and gloom. That is a path being paved by increasingly reckless billionaires and their profit margins. AI is capable of doing incredible things to serve communities and people, and to be done in a mindful and equitable way. In her book, Hao gives the example of Te Hiku Media, a Maui-owned broadcaster that set out to preserve their language but refused to let big tech extract their voice data. Instead, they built their own speech models and kept control in the hands of the community. "We think that we should empower the communities to lead their own platforms and solutions, so that they can work together and help to move their people forward. We've shown that actually in today's day and age, with the technology available, with the right skill set, you can actually do some impactful things, and you don't need to be saved by some big outsider who's got a lot of money." "The question isn't whether AI will shape our future. It's who gets to shape AI." "They have so much power. How can we stop them when it feels like even the very terms of our conversation about what they're doing are dependent on their actions?" "First of all, I think you're articulating something that is also central to empire building: empires make you feel like they're inevitable." "Right?" "But throughout history, every empire has fallen. They need to consume so much in order to continue that, when there starts to be resistance on all of the things they need to feed on to fortify the empire and perpetuate the empire, it starts to crumble."
