## Lines Drawn in the Sand

It started with Sykes-Picot, the agreement between the British and French to carve up the region in 1916. These two men devised a plan that would not only divide the spoils of the declining Ottoman Empire between the two European powers, but would draw lines in the sand that would ultimately influence the shape of the Middle East for the next century.

But it didn't end there. Through a series of negotiations, conventions, and the League of Nations mandates, British and French colonialism set many of the borders we see today.

The World Zionist Organization even got in on the action, since they saw Palestine as theirs following the British Balfour Declaration: the establishment in Palestine of a national home for the Jewish people.

The Zionists submitted their own border proposals to the British of what their future Jewish homeland should be.

The final border between Palestine and Lebanon came from the Paulet-Newcombe agreement in 1920, with complete disregard for the inhabitants of the land and their protests. These foreign powers drew lines in the sand that began the process of rupture that we feel today.
