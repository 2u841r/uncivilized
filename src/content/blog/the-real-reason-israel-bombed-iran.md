## A war 30 years in the making

It is 1992. In Jerusalem, a younger Benjamin Netanyahu was a member of Knesset for the Likud party. He makes what seems to be an outlandish prediction: Iran will have a nuclear bomb in the next 3 to 5 years. But at first, no one takes him seriously.

Meanwhile, the Israeli Labor Party is in the midst of negotiating a peace deal with the Palestinian Liberation Organization. The right wing is terrified of that prospect because it would ruin their vision for greater Israel. They wanted to spoil the talks. So they resort to fear-mongering to divert attention from peace with the Palestinians to a bigger, more dangerous enemy. And Iran is the perfect boogeyman.

As head of the conservative Likud party, Netanyahu himself became the face of the opposition to Oslo. But when that didn't work, the Israeli right assassinated Israeli Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin. Since then, Netanyahu and the Israeli right have utilized that tactic over and over and over again.

"Iran lied. If not stopped, Iran could produce a nuclear weapon in a very short time. It could be a year. It could be within a few months." "They have the wherewithal, the stored up preserved knowledge to make a bomb very quickly if they wanted to do it." "Iran is so dangerous. Weeks away from having the fissile material for an entire arsenal of nuclear bombs." "They're very close. They're 6 months away from being about 90% of having the enriched uranium for an atom bomb." "Iran is gearing up to produce 25 bombs, atomic bombs a year, 250 bombs in a decade." "Ladies and gentlemen, time is running out. Iran will be capable of producing, alone, without importing anything, nuclear bombs within 3 to 5 years."

Always the same thing. Iran is a few years away from a nuclear weapon, which as we know has been a lie. But what explains this obsession with Iran? Well, there are three reasons.

## Control

One, control. The psychology of Israeli society is built on fear. And the surest way to shift public opinion in Israel is to tell them they're on the brink of annihilation. And in the case of Iran, it's nuclear annihilation. Today, the Jewish state refuses to be a victim of a nuclear holocaust perpetrated by the Iranian regime.

Netanyahu has carefully and deliberately crafted the narrative since the 1990s, weaponizing the Israeli psychology of fear and the idea of a second Holocaust to paint himself as the protector of the Jewish state from impending doom. "A targeted military operation to roll back the Iranian threat to Israel's very survival." It has been in this way that he unites Israeli society behind him and gets them to fall in line. His government is about to collapse: Iran. Corruption charges: Iran. Two-state solution with the Palestinians, but Iran.

## Diversion

And this brings me to the second reason, diversion. Netanyahu has consistently manufactured the threat of Iran's nuclear bomb to divert the world's attention from Palestine, the same way he tried to do in the early 1990s. See, Netanyahu is the longest serving Israeli prime minister ever and has been in power for the majority of the Oslo negotiations era. He has done everything in his power to destroy the prospect of a Palestinian state. And during his tenure, Israel has only colonized more Palestinian land and constructed a system of apartheid in Palestine between the river and the sea.

And here's the thing: Netanyahu would almost refuse to speak about Palestine and Palestinians. Instead, he would focus on Iran at his speeches in the UN, in interviews with the media, and in his meetings with world leaders. This was deliberate. He wanted everyone to forget about Palestine, to relegate the Palestinian cause to a minority issue within Israel rather than a people fighting for liberation and self-determination. So he needed a credible and scary enemy to rally the world behind. And that was Iran.

This failed when October 7th and the genocide in Gaza made Palestine the number one issue in the world. And recently a tide was turning as Gaza was being starved. Countries that are some of Israel's closest friends were now being critical. There was talk about recognition of a Palestinian state and even the talk of sanctions, but that's when Netanyahu did what Netanyahu does best.

A few days later, Israel attacks Iran and these countries are now trumpeting the same old line: "Israel has a right to defend itself." And Netanyahu's tactic of diversion worked. Attention went from Gaza to Iran. "Moments ago, Israel launched Operation Rising Lion."
