On my way. Hi, this is Farah, an environmental activist from Jerusalem. She took me to the Old City to talk about fast fashion and overconsumption during Eid.

## The Fast Fashion Epidemic

Fast fashion is based on the concept of more and more and more, encouraging more consumption, and they do that by producing clothes in the cheapest way possible. Fast fashion is an epidemic across the world: people buy over 100 billion new pieces of clothing every year, that's 400% more than two decades ago. And all this consumption is happening at the expense of underpaid, exploited workers, usually in countries from the global South. It also comes at the expense of the environment; fashion is the third largest polluting industry in the world. Making this amount of clothing is very resource and energy intensive, and still three-fifths of the world's clothing ends up in landfills or incinerators less than a year after it's made. Every Eid, a Muslim holiday where it's customary to buy new clothes, consumption goes dramatically up. It is important to think about the cost of your new Eid outfit and how your decisions fit into the bigger picture.

"So this is also like a typical tourist shop that sells local things, traditional Palestinian things, but they're actually mass produced in other countries like China and Turkey and Bangladesh. So it's sold as local, but actually there's nothing local about it. I look at this and I feel so sad, not just because it's very ugly, but also that it has replaced everything that is local and pushed them out of the market, because they're not being able to compete price-wise."

## Slow Fashion

And so what's slow fashion, what's the movement to counteract that? "Slow fashion is kind of the opposite of fast fashion. It's all about producing clothes in a mindful and sustainable way. It's about valuing the artisans, encouraging local production and services."

"Deep inside every one of us, we're not comfortable with hyper-consumption culture. But we're so baked into it and it's surrounding us, so we can't really resist it, because it's like swimming against the stream." "And sometimes I think awareness..." "It's the fundamental thing, you need to work on the mindset. But in the end it's not everything; there needs to be real action as well. So capitalism, neoliberalism... Palestine has affected the relationship with the land, its relation to what we consume. But it also created this environment that is very much attuned to losing that, because that was brought in by the occupier as a way to sedate people, to get them more interested in how do I consume and how do I be within society, rather than how do I resist the system of oppression." "It's all intertwined. There's no one system; oppression is not isolated from others. It can't be."

## Occupation and the Old City

The Old City of Jerusalem is under military occupation. Israeli soldiers and settlers fill the city and have been actively trying to push Palestinians out for decades. The occupation is part of daily life here, and it impacts the livelihoods of these shopkeepers by suffocating the economy of the city.

"This is one of the souks in Old Jerusalem where most of its shops are closed. It's a typical representation of the difficulties that many shop owners in the Old City are facing. It's just too expensive to stay alive and to keep open."

"Sometimes I think it's easy for us to criticize that people are selling their embroidery at the bottom, and that they're getting things from China, because people want to survive as well. It's not an easy situation to be in. This is a global system, capitalism, hyper-consumption. How do you fight that on a local level?" "So I am fighting it both through my personal choices, personal consumption choices. But my individual actions are not enough, and therefore I try my best to spread inspiration through content creation, through blogging. That's why I created the Always Palestine platform, because I wanted to spread a message to a wider audience: that it is possible to be modern, young, and fashionable, without conforming to the system and without being a slave to the system."

## Making Things Last

A part of my lifestyle of not consuming and buying new things is that I take care of what I own and try to prolong its lifetime, so that I don't need to waste it and in order to avoid buying new stuff.

"So I got shirts; the shirts belonged to my mom when I was a kid and now I took them over. It has a small hole in it, so I need help with fixing it, because I couldn't do it alone."

"I learned a lot from my elderly relatives, because in reality they didn't have waste. Back then it was extremely minimal. They were appreciating the resources a lot more, not just because they were more costly and there was less income back then, but also because there wasn't this availability or supply of excess on the markets."

"It's not cool to consume. It's not cool to be like everyone else and try to buy new things all the time in order to show off and fill that gap that you have. It's more cool to have your individual values aligned with nature, that are not destructive to the environment and society."
