Blockbuster games like Call of Duty gave millions of players a hyper-realistic view into the American war in the Middle East, from special operations to assassinations to drone attacks. And on the psychological level, it created an us-versus-them mentality, with one side being good and the other being evil. This goes beyond representation. Studies have shown that players of these military games have a stronger acceptance of military interventions, making them more supportive of real-world policies like military occupation, invasion, and drone strikes.

The US Army sponsors tournaments to recruit the best players, and then uses the same technology in these games to build training simulators, especially for drones. Drone pilots often describe their jobs as eerily similar to playing a video game. Gaming skills learned in civilian life directly translate into the military, like piloting unmanned drones.

This gamification of warfare isn't just a coincidence, it's by design. These video games are a powerful tool in mass manipulation and shifting public opinion. They have desensitized people to warfare, and we are now seeing that play out in Palestine and Lebanon.
