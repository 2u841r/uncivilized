the watermelon wasn't always an
anti-black racist strope in the US it
originally had a different meaning
similar to the symbolism of the
watermelon in the Palestinian movement
for Liberation it represented the
freedom and emancipation of black people
from slavery but that didn't last
because any attempt at black
self-determination and Independence was
met with opposition and violence this is
a story of the fragility of Supremacy
and to tell it we need to go back in
time to the end of the American Civil
War following emancipation and the end
of slavery in the US in the 1860s many
Southern African-Americans refused to
work or share crop on white own Farms
they began cultivating their own land
many grew and sold water melons and
started to develop their own economic
and financial Independence the fruit
became a material symbol of their
freedom this Independence infuriated
southern white people because it
threatened their racial order remember a
large part of their ideology was that
black people were not ready for freedom
and that was being proven wrong AED and
embedded by watermelon this is when they
decided to unleash their Insidious plan
they wanted to take this image of
freedom and destroy it so they decided
to weaponize the watermelon into an ugly
stereotype through a deliberate and
elaborate narrative they aimed to
reinforce the idea that black people
were lesser than and inherently
incapable of being independent human
beings and it went as follows one black
people were lazy this was represented by
how easy it was to grow watermelons
because they didn't require much work
The Stereotype was further reinforced by
the image that because watermelons were
hard to eat black people would be
consumed with eating them rather than
doing any work at all two black people
were unclean this was shown through
exaggerated depictions of how messy it
is to eat a watermelon the subtext
behind this was purity of race and that
this one was not
pure three that black people are
childlike with the implication that they
needed overseers or Masters this was
shown by suggesting that a watermelon
was a fruit only a child would like
because it is sweet colorful and devoid
of much nutritional value this narrative
fueled by these racist stereotypes blew
up and really took off in the 1900s it
was part of the popular culture and
showed up in songs minstril posters
postcards books TV shows and films it
persists to this day even the first
black president was a target of it
cultural symbols have immense power to
shape how we see the world and the
people in it that's why stories and
narratives matter they took what was
once a symbol of freedom and turned it
into a stereotype to reinforce racial
Supremacy and hierarchy while completely
erasing its history because how fragile
do you have to be to feel threatened by
seeing other people enjoying their
Newfound Freedom living off land they
owned and becoming financially
independent by selling watermelon the
answer is very threatened as
Palestinians we have also adopted the
watermelon as a symbol of our own
struggle for freedom because it colors
match our flag that was banned by Israel
their fragility is triggered from their
drive to erase Palestinians and replace
the native with the settler so any sign
or symbol of our Identity or presence
becomes an existential threat to them
and their ideology so instead of
carrying the flag people started
carrying watermelons and now it's
everywhere the watermelon is only one of
many connections between the black and
Palestinian Liberation struggles Sol ity
