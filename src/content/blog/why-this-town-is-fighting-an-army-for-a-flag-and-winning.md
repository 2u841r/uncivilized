This is the Palestinian flag. I'm sure you've seen it before, and you'd probably think you could fly it all over Palestine. Well, think again. There's a war being waged over this flag throughout the country. One town in particular has seen this battle unfold: the town of Hawara has become a battleground. Palestinians are confronting Israeli settlers and soldiers over who has the right to fly which flag. But this is part of a bigger story, one of colonization, identity, and resistance.

Our journey starts not too long ago in a Palestinian town in the northern part of the West Bank. A Jewish Israeli settler was filmed climbing up a pole to take down a Palestinian flag as he was driving by. So naturally, a Palestinian man put it back up. But this time, Israeli soldiers arrived and removed the flag. This happened over and over and over again. The flags were taken down, broken, and confiscated. Palestinians were beaten and arrested, and the city was shut down and occupied. All of this over a flag. And every time there was an act of Israeli oppression, it was met with Palestinian resistance. But in the meantime, Israeli soldiers were protecting Israeli settlers as they were conducting a march to fly the Israeli flag.

Now you might ask yourself, what are these Israeli settlers and soldiers doing there in the first place, and why do they have the authority to take down a Palestinian flag flying in a Palestinian city but still be able to fly their own? I ask myself these questions all the time. I am Salem, a Palestinian political activist that lives in the West Bank, and I want to use my channel and platform to share about my home and our struggle for liberation through stories that connect people. And to be able to answer these questions, we need to go back in time to talk about colonization.

## The Colonization of Palestine

"It's the custom for our young people to move on in groups to make the new settlements." The colonization of Palestine is a process of erasure that has been taking place for over a century. "500 new immigrants from over 20 countries arrived in Israel in the span of just seven days." It is a historical continuum that moves in one direction, and it's rooted in partition, which is the division and then the theft of the land in increments, one step at a time. But Israeli settler colonialism doesn't stop there, because it can't fully flourish until it erases the people, their identity, and their symbols.

And that includes a symbol like the Palestinian flag, which stands for national liberation. With every increment of land stolen, an area it controls, Israel seeks to replace anything native with everything settler. This includes brutally assaulting anyone who dares to fly the Palestinian flag, but protects and safeguards any display of the Israeli flag. And that extends to '48 Palestine, Israel, Jerusalem, and now the West Bank. The West Bank is the final frontier of the vision for Greater Israel sponsored by Zionism.

Let's go back to the 90s for a second. During the Oslo Peace Accords, a future Palestinian state on 22 percent of historic Palestine was further partitioned into three areas. Area A, 18 percent of the West Bank, was under full Palestinian control. Area B, 22 percent of the West Bank, was under partial Palestinian control. And Area C, 60 percent of the West Bank, was under full Israeli control. And because of undeterred illegal Israeli settlement construction, this is what that potential Palestinian state looks like today. Isn't it funny how partition seems to work out and serve the colonizer but not the colonized? We basically fully control only 18 of 22 percent of our historic homeland. That's a lot of math to basically say we just got screwed.

## Back to Hawara

So let's go back to Hawara, where we started. It's in one of those enclaves that is supposedly under Palestinian control. There's a road that cuts through the town that is used by these illegal Israeli settlers to get to their Israeli-only communities in the north of the West Bank. As they drive this road, they come across Palestinian flags and displays of Palestinian identity, and that is too much for their colonial sensibilities to handle. So they get out of the car and they take the flag down. Why? Because they can, and they have the backing of the Israeli military.

To clarify, the Israeli military is the supreme authority in the West Bank, not the Palestinian government. We are under a military occupation, and so any form of Palestinian sovereignty is actually an illusion. That's why we can't fly our flags in our cities without being assaulted by Israeli settlers and soldiers who occupy us, and this is the case across all of historic Palestine.

The Palestinian flag is a symbol of resistance in the face of this erasure. It says: despite the apartheid, the ethnic cleansing, the displacement, the assaults, the arrests, the incursions, the colonization, we are here and we are not going anywhere. This is why any form of its display cannot be tolerated by this regime. This is the battle of the flags, and this is why we must keep flying it.
